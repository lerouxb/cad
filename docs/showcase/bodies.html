Bodies live with their step

Still one timeline — but a body appears next to the step that created it, not in a separate browser. That is the fix for Fusion's split: there, the object list is disjointed from the timeline, so it is unclear that the bodies are the things as they exist at that step. Here the origin is explicit, and rolling back visibly pauses the steps — and therefore the bodies — that no longer exist.

What this study tests

  • The step is the step; the output is the thing. Every step lists what it creates — a sketch, a datum plane or a body — so the chip is where you edit that thing, not the step.
  • One step can make several bodies. One sketch, two disjoint profiles, one extrude → + body, + lid. Bodies that share an edge fuse; disjoint ones stay separate.
  • Later steps reference, not create. The boss joins → body; the hole cuts → lid. The chip stays at the creating step.
  • Selecting a body highlights it — a quiet highlighter wash on the solid, not just the chip — and opens its popover (appearance, visibility, isolate).
  • Double-click a chip to rename it, exactly like a timeline step name. Sketches and planes rename the same way.
  • Consuming a body strikes it through. “merge lid” shows → body and a struck-through ✕ lid — visual only, not selectable.
  • Groups live in the timeline. A group draws a bracket across its steps with the name and a − to collapse. Collapsed, it becomes a stack chip (mark, name, count) that you click to expand — per group, not a global toggle. Collapse is refused while the active step is inside (roll back first).
  • Drag to group. Drag a step onto an adjacent step and they become a group. Drop a step onto the edge of a group and it joins. Drag a step out of a group and it leaves, landing just before or after the group.
  • A one-step group dissolves. When the second-to-last step leaves, the group disappears and the remaining step is alone and ungrouped.
  • Groups are not selectable. Only steps are. A group can be named (double-click), collapsed, expanded or ungrouped — nothing else. No nesting.
  • Identity breaks visibly. “Connect the profiles” merges the rectangles into one body, so the cut that targeted lid loses its body — ink, a dotted rule and a “!”, with reselect/open sketch, exactly like a lost face.

Open questions

  • Deliberate merges. If a sketch edit joins two bodies on purpose, that is not a break — the user changed their mind. The lever is probably the feature's operation (join vs new body), and a downstream ref that loses its body gets reselect. Whether that is enough needs a real example; defer.
  • Default operation. A new extrude should default to join (Fusion) and fall back to new body when the result cannot be joined (no body to join, or disjoint). Is that the right fallback, or should it ask?
  • Do the steps show only bodies, or sketches and planes too? (This study says all three.)
  • Is the body name editable in the chip (double-click) or only in the popover? Do names renumber when a feature is suppressed?
  • Collapsed group: is a count + stack + name enough, or does it need a peek (a body count, a mini stack of the bodies it made)?
Components are deliberately out of scope: a component is a namespaced set of bodies with its own timeline, which is a separate model. The only thing to protect now is the seam — a body id should be namespacable later (componentId/bodyId).
