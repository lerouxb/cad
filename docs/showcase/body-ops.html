Combine and split

The Solid tab's body operations all share one shape: pick a target, pick a tool, choose what to do, and see the result before committing. This study reuses the sketch preview grammar for solids.

What each op does

  • Combine · join unions the tool into the target (here a boss on the plate).
  • Combine · cut removes the tool from the target (here a through hole).
  • Combine · intersect keeps only the overlap.
  • Keep tool bodies leaves the tool as a separate body; otherwise it is consumed. The kept tool is tinted, the consumed one is dashed.
  • Split body cuts one body into two with an existing splitting tool — a datum plane, a planar face, or another body — and keep both controls whether the far half survives.
  • The position lives on the plane, not the split. Choosing Plane 1 shows its exact offset (40.0 mm), editable as a number. If you want an arbitrary position, you create a datum plane there and split with it — the split never owns a free-form position.

Open questions

  • Are target/tool a selection in the dialog, or a bodies list on the document? How do the result bodies keep stable identity across a split?
  • What happens when the splitting plane or face is deleted or its reference is lost — does the split error visibly, or fall back?
  • Does intersect need a distinct icon/colour so it is not mistaken for cut?
  • Should the tool body stay ghosted after apply when it is consumed?
This is stylised geometry for a design decision, not a real B-rep. The point is the interaction: target, tool, operation, and a live preview.
