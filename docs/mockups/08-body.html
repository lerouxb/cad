Bodies have looks

Body appearance is a property of the body, and the body is first created by a feature. Selecting that feature exposes the body: its material and finish sit next to the feature parameters, the same way the name does.

What this study tests

  • Appearance is not a global settings panel. It lives on the body-creating step.
  • Later features keep the body. A join or cut changes the shape but not the material; the body stays warm grey.
  • Material and finish are separate. Material changes the neutral tint; finish is a surface property for later rendering and print planning.
  • The timeline detail is the summary. The body row shows the current look; the panel is the transient editor.

Open questions

  • Should body appearance be editable from any step once the body exists, or only from the creating step?
  • How does this extend to multiple bodies: one appearance per body, or per-body material assignments in an assembly?
  • Do material and finish belong in the document, or are they presentation-only?
The appearance panel is deliberately small: one body, two decisions (material, finish), one place.
