History is the workspace

The timeline is not a status bar bolted onto the bottom. It is the main navigation surface: the model is always shown at the active step, and editing a step is the same gesture as scrubbing history.

What this study tests

  • Rollback is a visible state. Steps 05–07 stay in place, dimmed and italic, rather than disappearing.
  • The highlighter marks the present. Only the active step gets the marker; the rest is ink on paper.
  • The stage follows the timeline. The body is shown as it existed at step 04: no gusset, no holes.
  • The stage has no title. The active step name above is the identity; the stage is only the model at that step. The header keeps the document name and the next action.
  • Steps are named. The active step name is the handle. The detail line offers “rename”, because in a complicated history the name is how you identify the right sketch when a reference breaks.
  • One contextual action. “Roll forward” is the only primary action because it is the only next decision.

Open questions

  • Should nested sketches sit under their feature, or stay flat like this?
  • Does a dimmed-paused step read clearly enough without a lock or eye icon?
  • How should dragging the rollback bar behave on a touch device?
Paper is the default. Night mode is the same ink and the same single highlighter, inverted.
